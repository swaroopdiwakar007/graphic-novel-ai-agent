# Panel templates

A catalog of page layouts. Name the layout in each page script (`Layout: hero-row`) so assembly knows how to place the panels. The visual catalog is in `docs/images/panel-templates.png`.

| Key | Panels | Good for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `strip-3` | 3 | gags, quiet beats, simple exchanges | classic three-beat rhythm: setup, build, payoff |
| `grid-4` | 4 | conversations, steady pacing | even panels feel calm and orderly |
| `grid-6` | 6 | dense or fast scenes | keep each panel to one action or one line |
| `splash` | 1 + inset | the biggest moment of a chapter | at most one or two per book |
| `hero-row` | 4 | scene setting, then reaction | wide establishing top, two mids, wide closer |
| `angled` | 4 | action, tension, scene changes | slanted gutters add energy; keep reading order obvious |
| `wide-4` | 4 | cinematic, slow, atmospheric | stacked wide panels feel like film frames |
| `webtoon` | any | vertical-scroll formats | panel height sets the pace; gaps work as pauses |

## Choosing a layout
- Quiet or emotional beat: fewer, larger panels (`wide-4`, `strip-3`).
- Fast or chaotic beat: more, smaller, angled panels (`grid-6`, `angled`).
- Reveal or turning point: `splash`, ideally on a right-hand page so the page turn builds suspense.
- Vary layouts across pages; repeating one grid for ten pages feels flat.

## Panel shapes and borders
- Rectangular: default, clear and calm.
- Angled or geometric: energy and disruption.
- Borderless or full-bleed: dream, memory, open space.
- Keep gutters consistent within a page.

## Title and credits box
The first page may reserve a strip for title and author; the layout then loses one row. Note it in the script header.

## Lettering elements
| Element | Use |
|---|---|
| Speech balloon | normal dialogue; tail points at the speaker |
| Thought cloud | inner thoughts |
| Shout (jagged) | yelling, alarms |
| Whisper (dashed) | quiet speech |
| Caption box | narration, time and place |
| SFX | sound effects, drawn as part of the art |

Read order inside a panel: top-left to bottom-right. Leave empty space in the artwork where text will sit.
